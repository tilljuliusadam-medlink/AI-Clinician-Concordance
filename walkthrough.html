Replication package - how it works

Eight Python files, 1714 lines, one command. Every number below was measured on a run, not estimated.

This package is FULLY SIMULATED. No real patient data: the cohort, the expert ratings, and every number in results/ are generated, and no result here is a study finding.

main.py runs those three in order. The data are a fixed file, generated once and committed, so two runs give identical output.

2. The exposure: one flat two-by-two table per patient

per patient, over all 18 options at once:

                     clinician chose    clinician did not
  AI recommended           TP                  FP
  AI did not               FN                  TN          TP+FP+FN+TN = 18

  F1                = 2TP / (2TP + FP + FN)
  balanced accuracy = (recall + specificity) / 2
  PABAK             = 2 x accuracy - 1

Every option lands in one cell of one table. There is no per-family score and no averaging of family scores, which would be a different quantity. The five drug families Table 1 reports are derived afterwards as the union of their options, so a family can never contradict its own members.

Why F1 shrinks the sample and the other two metrics do not

F1 is undefined when a patient has no true positive, meaning the AI and the clinician never agreed on a single option. Those patients are dropped from the F1 analyses and kept for balanced accuracy and PABAK, which are computable for everyone. Here that is 454 of 2000 patients, 22.7%, which is why the F1 blocks run on 1546 rows and the others on 2000.

3. The six analysis blocks

One routine runs all six. Each fits 13 binary outcomes by logistic regression and 7 continuous outcomes by linear regression, with the concordance metric as a continuous predictor and six covariates: sex, age, race, number of psychiatric diagnoses, months to follow-up, AI confidence level.

The three rules applied on top of every fit
  • Weighted and unweighted are one code path, with one deliberate branch. sm.GLM(..., var_weights=w) and sm.WLS(..., weights=w), with w a vector of ones when the block is unweighted, which is mathematically identical to no weights. Block 6 post-stratifies to the 2022 American Community Survey in cells of age group x sex x race: 1898 of 2000 patients match a cell, weights run 0.31 to 16.99, effective sample size 966. The branch is the covariance estimator: post-stratification weights are sampling weights, not variance weights, so a weighted block fits with cov_type="HC0", the linearization (survey) sandwich, and an unweighted block keeps the model-based one. The Model column of every row records which was used.
  • Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate correction, within each block: one predictor in one sample defines one family, so the 13 binary and the 7 continuous regressions are corrected together as 20 tests. Raw and corrected p both reported.
  • E-values (VanderWeele and Ding 2017): how strong an unmeasured confounder would have to be to explain the association away. Binary outcomes convert the odds ratio to an approximate risk ratio first, by square root when the outcome occurs in at least 15% of the sample and unchanged otherwise. Continuous outcomes map the coefficient through exp(0.91 x beta / SD).

4. The expert sub-study

The main analysis leaves one question open. In a low-concordance case the AI plan was never carried out, so a bad 12-month outcome cannot show that the AI plan would have been better, and low concordance may only mark a harder case. This sub-study puts that question to human judgment. Board-certified psychiatrists with at least 10 years of experience after certification, none of them affiliated with Mayo Clinic or MedLink Global, rate 300 cases: 240 with low treatment concordance (F1 below 0.50 and PABAK below 0.70) and an unfavorable 12-month outcome (death, suicidality, hospitalization, emergency room visit), plus 60 high-concordance cases (F1 0.70 to 1.00) so the panel does not see poor outcomes only. Two reviewers rate each case independently, blinded to which of the two plans is the AI's: case complexity, then the appropriateness of the diagnostic assessment and of the treatment plan on a 1-4 scale, then a forced choice between the two. The difference between the two appropriateness ratings is the unit of analysis.

d = AI rating - clinician rating         one row, integer -3 to +3

d ~ 1 + (1|case) + (1|reviewer)          crossed random intercepts, REML

non-inferiority : lower bound of the 95%   CI  >=  -0.5
superiority     : lower bound of the 97.5% CI  >=   0
